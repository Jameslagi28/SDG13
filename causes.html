Causes of Deforestation

Understanding the main drivers behind forest loss in the Philippines, from illegal activities to economic pressures that threaten our remaining forests.

Illegal Logging

Illegal logging has destroyed multiple forests in the Philippines, particularly in Cagayan and Sierra Madre. In 2020, it was identified as a significant cause of severe flooding during Typhoon Ulysses.

Authorities have confiscated 57,000 board feet of illegally cut wood in Cagayan alone. However, despite ongoing efforts to combat this issue, illegal logging continues to threaten our forests.

Impact Example:
Typhoon Ulysses flooding in 2020 was worsened by illegal logging activities that removed natural flood barriers.

Mining site in Palawan

Mining Activities

Mining operations in Palawan have cleared large forest areas, creating bald spots that are visible from a distance. These operations threaten what many call the country’s “last ecological frontier.”

Although Palawan declared a 50-year moratorium on new permits in 2025, six active mining sites continue to threaten forests, rivers, and wildlife habitats.

Recent Development:
Palawan’s 50-year mining moratorium (2025) aims to protect remaining forests, but existing operations continue.

Agricultural Expansion

Deforestation is mostly caused by commercial farming for crops like bananas and palm oil, as well as traditional kaingin practices. Large-scale agriculture requires clearing vast forest areas for plantations.

In Mindoro, forests that once provided habitat for the tamaraw and bleeding-heart pigeon have been cleared for agriculture and settlements, resulting in the destruction of critical habitats.

Mindoro Case Study:
Between 2003 and 2015, Mindoro lost more than 200,000 hectares of forest cover to agricultural expansion.

Urban Expansion in Rizal

Urbanization & Infrastructure

Rapid urbanization and population growth have increased flooding risks in Rizal and other provinces near Metro Manila. The conversion of forests to settlements and commercial districts removes natural flood barriers.

The Marikina River Basin's natural capacity to absorb rainfall was diminished by inadequate land use planning, resulting in severe flooding during Typhoon Enteng in 2024.

Expert Warning:
Paved land and unchecked development continue to cause long-term environmental harm despite reforestation efforts.
