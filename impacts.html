Environmental Impacts

Understanding the devastating consequences of deforestation on our environment and communities

Environmental Consequences

Soil Erosion

Soil erosion removes the top layer of soil where plants obtain most of their water and nutrients. When this fertile topsoil slides away, land productivity declines and farmers lose essential resources for food production.

roded soil washes into rivers and streams, creating thick sediment that disrupts water flow and can result in flooding. This process becomes cyclical once it starts.

Biodiversity Loss

Forests are home to unique plants and animals found only in the Philippines. When forests disappear, entire species lose their habitats and face extinction.

Critical habitats for endangered species like the tamaraw and bleeding-heart pigeon have been destroyed, threatening the country's rich biodiversity.

Increased Flooding

Deforestation removes natural barriers that absorb rainfall and prevent floods. Without forest cover, rainwater flows directly into rivers, causing severe flooding.

The 2020 flooding during Typhoon Ulysses was significantly worsened by illegal logging in affected areas, demonstrating the direct link between deforestation and disaster severity.

Climate Impact

Forests store carbon and help regulate climate. When destroyed, they release stored carbon into the atmosphere, contributing to climate change and global warming.

Loss of forest cover also reduces the land's ability to moderate temperature and humidity, leading to more extreme weather patterns and stronger typhoons.

Impact on Communities

Displacement

Communities lose their homes and traditional lands to deforestation activities

Food Insecurity

Loss of fertile soil and forest resources threatens food production and security

Water Scarcity

Forests protect watersheds; their loss leads to water shortage and contamination

A Global Challenge

Soil erosion is not just a local issue—it's a global challenge. Soil is eroding faster than it can be replenished, making land less suitable for farming worldwide. This is especially alarming with the world population projected to exceed 9 billion by mid-century.

The Philippines' deforestation contributes to this global crisis, making urgent action essential for both local communities and international environmental stability.
